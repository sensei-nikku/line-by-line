## Track 0: Paid in Full

On Monday night, Terrence went back to the library with his notebook, the three reports, and the letter from Anchor Recovery.

Over the weekend, he had sorted every line into two lists, True and Wrong. The True list held the Desert Sun repossession and the Rapid Cash inquiry. Those lines hurt, but they were his. The Wrong list ran bureau by bureau.

On Experian, Meridian's $310 was under $500, so it should not have been there at all, and Phoenix General and Sierra Asset were the same $840 bill, listed twice. On Equifax, Anchor Recovery's $1,900 was still there even though it was paid. So was Meridian's $310, along with an address in Fresno, California, where he had never lived. TransUnion had Anchor Recovery's $1,900 and Meridian's $310.

He filed a dispute with each bureau for every line on the Wrong list, kept each explanation short, and uploaded a photo of the Anchor Recovery letter as proof. Each site gave him a confirmation number and the same message: Most disputes are completed within 30 days.

At home, he pulled last month's Copper State Visa statement from the file box. He had already paid it, and across the top, in pencil, he had written: Paid in full, August tenth.

[[The statement said: *Closing date: July 18. New balance: $340. Minimum payment due: $35. Payment due date: August 12. Credit limit: $1,200. Purchase APR: 23.99%.*]]{{He went down the statement one line at a time. The closing date was July eighteenth, and the new balance was $340. The minimum payment was $35, due by August twelfth. His credit limit was $1,200, and the purchase APR was 23.99%.}}

Near the bottom was a box with a bold heading. It said: *Minimum Payment Warning. If you make only the minimum payment each period, you will pay more in interest and it will take you longer to pay off your balance.*

Something didn't add up. He had paid this card to zero, yet all three reports said he owed $340.

On Tuesday at lunch, he called Valley Credit Union from the boiler room and asked for Ruth Okafor, the loan officer who had helped him the week before.

Ruth picked up on the second ring. @Ruth"This is Ruth."

@Terrence"Ruth, it's Terrence Whitfield. I filed those disputes last night, with all three bureaus."

[[Ruth said, ]]@Ruth"Good. Now you wait about thirty days, and you don't apply for that car loan until you see what comes back."

Terrence ran his thumb down the statement to the rate. @Terrence"It says my APR is 23.99%. I've had this card eight years, and I pay the whole balance every month. Have I been paying that rate the whole time?"

[[Ruth said, ]]@Ruth"Not if you pay the full statement balance by the due date. Then you've never paid a cent of interest. That's the grace period. Miss it once, and the interest starts."

His eyes went to the box at the bottom of the page. @Terrence"Then who is this warning for?"

[[Ruth said, ]]@Ruth"People who only pay the $35. The law makes the bank print it. The minimum keeps you in good standing, but not out of interest."

[[Terrence said, ]]@Terrence"Here's what I don't get. I paid it to zero on August tenth, and two days later, the reports said I owed $340."

Ruth's chair creaked on the other end of the line. @Ruth"Your card company reports your balance once a month, from the end of your billing cycle. That's your closing date. By the time you paid, that number had already gone out."

Terrence found the closing date on the statement: July eighteenth. @Terrence"So if I pay before the eighteenth, the bureaus see a smaller number."

[[Ruth said, ]]@Ruth"Same money. Different day."

[[Terrence said, ]]@Terrence"Then I'll cancel the Summit card while I'm at it. I never use it, and the rate on it is 31.99%."

Ruth cut in. @Ruth"Don't. Right now your two limits add up to $1,700. Cancel the Summit card, and every bureau sees $1,200. TransUnion already made that mistake. Don't make it on purpose. Keep the card you don't use, and pay the other one in full. That's the whole trick."

After the call, Terrence sat with the statement for a while. The Visa charged 23.99% and the store card charged 31.99%, and he had never paid a dollar of interest on either. Somebody was paying for these cards.

He folded the statement and slid it into his pocket. @Terrence"If it isn't me, then who's paying for my cards?"
