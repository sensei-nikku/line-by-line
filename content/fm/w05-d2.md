## Track 1: Part 4, Food and Health

Card 4A is groceries. You don't need to look anything up. The table is printed on the card.

First, pick one plan for your household: low-cost, moderate, or liberal. Low-cost means you cook most meals and shop smart. Liberal means more brand names and convenience food.

Then add one amount for each person who eats at home. That's you, your partner, your kids, and any adult you support who lives with you. Use the man, woman, or child row for each person.

Last, multiply your total by the household-size number. One person, times 1.2. Two people, times 1.1. Three people, times 1.05. Four or more, no change.

Jordan picked low-cost, because he cooks now. One man is $316. Times 1.2, that's $379. That's GR.

[PAUSE: Do card 4A.]

Card 4B is health, child care, and basics. This one is a lookup. Scan the code, or search "MIT Living Wage Calculator."

Type your city in the search box, and pick your metro area. Scroll down to the table called Typical Expenses. Find your column. It's on the Plan card in Monday's packet.

Copy three rows: Medical, Child Care, and Other. Each one is a yearly number, so divide each one by 12. Then add the three.

Jordan's Medical row is $3,008 a year. Divided by 12, that's $251. His Child Care is $0, because he has no kids. His Other row is $4,992. Divided by 12, that's $416. Together, that's $667. That's MT.

City not on the MIT site? Use the nearest metro area in the same state. The Stuck card has more help.

## Track 2: Part 4, People, Phone, and Wants

Card 4C is adults you support. First, the yes-or-no question: do you support an adult who isn't your partner? If not, write $0 and go to 4D.

If you do, fill in only the row that matches where they live.

If they live with you, go back to the MIT page. Use the 1 adult, 0 children column. Add Medical and Other, then divide by 12.

If they're in paid care, use your share of a monthly price for your city. The card has Phoenix prices. Assisted living is about $6,300 a month. Another city? Search "assisted living cost" and the city.

If they live somewhere else, write the money you send each month. Child support you pay goes here too.

The example on the card is Dana's mom, who lives with Dana. That's $667 a month. Jordan doesn't support anyone, so his AC is $0.

[PAUSE: Do card 4C.]

Card 4D is your phone and your minimum payments. Use your own phone bill. Count every line you pay for. Then add the minimum payment on each credit card or loan you're paying now. No debt? Write zero.

Jordan's phone is $40. His card's minimum payment is $94. Together, that's $134. That's PD.

[PAUSE: Do card 4D.]

Card 4E is Lifestyle and Discretionary. Lifestyle is the wants that are part of every month. Discretionary is free money for whatever comes up.

Be honest here. A zero on something you really spend money on is worse than an honest number.

Jordan's Lifestyle is eating out, his gym, his cooking class, and the Friday burritos. The burritos are $66 a week, times 4, so $264. His Lifestyle total is $453. His Discretionary is $100 for fun and $50 for gifts, so $150.

## Track 3: Part 5, Find What's Left

Part 5 is the analysis. This is where every dollar gets its orders.

Step 1 is pile 1, your Essentials. Copy each number from its box, and add down. HB and TB come from Monday's packet. GR, MT, AC, and PD come from Part 4.

Then count your help booth marks: the launch packet, Monday, and today. Each mark is $10 a month. Jordan didn't use the help booth, so his is $0.

Jordan's Essentials come to $2,745. That's ES.

[PAUSE: Do Step 1.]

Step 2 is what's left. Start with your monthly take-home, MB, from Monday. Subtract your Essentials. Subtract Lifestyle. Subtract Discretionary. What's left is L.

Jordan starts with $4,691. After all three, he has $1,343 left.

L can be negative. That's not a mistake. It means your budget is short, and you'll fix it in Step 3.

[PAUSE: Do Step 2.]

Step 3 is only for you if L is negative. If L is zero or more, skip to Step 4.

If you're short, cut from the bottom up. Start with Discretionary. Then Lifestyle. Then the biggest Essentials, like a cheaper apartment, a roommate, or no car. Keep cutting until what you saved is at least as big as what you were short.

Jordan's examples are from last month. He cut delivery from $612 to $250. Then he left the $1,725 apartment for one at $1,195.

When you're done, fix the matching numbers in Steps 1 and 2. Your Final Budget Card has to show the numbers after the cuts.

[PAUSE: Do Step 3, or skip it.]

Then play the next track.

## Track 4: Part 5, Save and Balance

Step 4 is pile 2, Security. That's your emergency fund.

Your goal is three months of Essentials, so ES times 3. If money is tight, start with $1,000 as your goal instead.

Then pick a monthly amount you can live with. That's SC. To see how long it takes, divide your goal by SC.

Jordan's goal is $8,235. He saves $350 a month. That's about 24 months, or two years.

[PAUSE: Do Step 4.]

Step 5 is pile 3, Goals. Name one goal with a price, like a car, a move, a certificate, or paying off a card.

Goals gets the rest of what's left: L minus SC. That's GL. To see how long your goal takes, divide its cost by GL.

Jordan's goal is paying off his card. It's $3,912. His GL is $993 a month, so it's gone in about four months.

[PAUSE: Do Step 5.]

Step 6 is the balance. Add all five piles. Then subtract that total from your take-home. The answer has to be exactly $0.

Not zero? Move dollars between Security and Goals until it is. If you're still short, go back and cut more.

Jordan's five piles add up to $4,691, his whole take-home. His balance is $0.

[PAUSE: Do Step 6.]

Then play the last track.

## Track 5: Part 5, Check It

Step 7 is each pile's share. For each pile, take the amount, divide by your take-home, MB, and multiply by 100. Round to a whole number.

Then find each pile out of every $10. That's just the share divided by 10.

Last, copy your target from the Target card in your launch packet, and compare.

Jordan's Essentials are 59%, so $5.90 out of every $10. He aimed for $6. But his Goals got twice his target, because he wants that card gone.

[PAUSE: Do Step 7.]

Step 8 is two reality checks. First, your housing share: HB, divided by MB, times 100. Under 30% is comfortable. 30 to 40% is stretched. Over 40% is trouble.

Second, your Essentials share: ES, divided by MB, times 100. The 50/30/20 rule aims for about 50%.

Jordan's housing is 31%, a little stretched. His Essentials are 59%, more than the rule. Write which check worries you most, and why.

[PAUSE: Do Step 8.]

Last is the Final Budget Card. Copy your final numbers, after any cuts. Everything on tomorrow's poster comes from this card, so bring it with you.

You sorted every dollar, and your budget balances. That's the hardest day of the project. Nice work.
