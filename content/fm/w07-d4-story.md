## Track 0: Five in the Morning

Terrence picked the snowball. He wanted Sierra Asset's name off his report before anything else, and he wanted to see a debt hit zero soon.

Ruth Okafor would have picked the avalanche. She told him so across her desk, and then she told him it didn't matter, as long as he finished. He paid off Meridian in December and the small loan in February. The last payment to Sierra Asset went out in April. He wrote each date in his notebook and drew a line through the debt.

Every Sunday night, he pulled his three reports at the library, the way Ruth had taught him. Nothing new showed up that he didn't recognize.

In April, he had made six car payments, all on time. He went back to the credit union with his latest scores and the notebook. Ruth looked at both, ran his file again, and lowered the rate on his car loan from 15.49% to 11.49%.

@Ruth"That's what six months of boring looks like. Keep being boring."

The Desert Sun line was still on his reports. A lawyer at the legal aid office went over the dates with him. She told him when his clock had started, and what not to say if a collector called. He wrote that date in his notebook too, on the page that said True. It would leave his reports in 2029. He knew the month.

One morning in May, he was driving to the west-side school at five o'clock. At the bus stop on the corner, someone was waiting in the dark with a district lanyard around his neck. It was Abdi, the new hire on the early crew. Abdi's shift started at five thirty, and his first bus didn't come until after five.

Terrence pulled over and rolled down the window.

@Terrence"So if I'm going that way anyway, you're riding with me."

Abdi got in. They rode the rest of the way without saying much. The sky over the west side was just starting to turn gray.

That night, Terrence opened his notebook to a clean page. He didn't write a number at the top this time. He wrote a name, and a time: Abdi, five o'clock.
