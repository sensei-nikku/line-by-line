## Track 0: Cash Today

On the first of October, Terrence paid the Visa in full. He had run the numbers both ways, and the minimum lost. The $1,040 came out of his car fund, and the fund dropped from $1,800 to $760.

A week later, a teacher at the west-side school put a sign in the window of his car. It was a 2015 Corolla with 96,000 miles, and he wanted $6,300 for it. He was moving to Oregon at the end of the month, and he said he would hold the car for Terrence until Saturday.

Terrence called Valley Credit Union from the parking lot and asked for Ruth Okafor, the loan officer who had been helping him all fall.

[[Ruth said, ]]@Ruth"With your scores where they are today, we can lend 80% of the price. That means 20% down. On $6,300, that's $1,260."

Terrence worked it out in his notebook. He had $760, so he was $500 short, and he had four days.

On the bus home, he passed the strip mall with the check casher. The Rapid Cash store was still there, two doors down, with a new banner across the front window. It said: *Cash for your title. Keep driving your car.* Terrence had no car to drive and no title to hand over. For the first time all fall, that felt like luck.

That evening, the mail held an orange envelope from Rapid Cash Online. Terrence knew the name. Last September, when his old car needed brakes, he had filled out a form at the Rapid Cash store next door to the check casher. Then he had walked out without signing.

[[The flyer said: *Terrence, you're pre-approved! Get up to $500 today. Fee: just $15 for every $100 borrowed, due in 14 days. Apply online in minutes.*]]{{The flyer said he was pre-approved for up to $500 today. The fee was just $15 for every $100 he borrowed, due in fourteen days, and he could apply online in minutes.}}

He went to the hall closet and found the folder from last September. The form was still in it, unsigned.

[[It said: *Title loan. Amount: $500. Monthly finance charge: 17%, or $85. Annual percentage rate: 204%. Your vehicle title will be held as security.*]]{{The form was for a title loan of $500. The monthly finance charge was 17%, which came to $85, and the annual percentage rate was 204%. They would have held his car title as security.}}

Then he turned over his Visa statement. On the back, it said that a cash advance cost a 5% fee, and that cash was charged 29.99% a year, with no grace period.

On Tuesday at lunch, he called Ruth from the boiler room.

@Terrence"Ruth, I've got three ways to get $500 by Saturday, and I don't like any of them."

He told her about the flyer, the old title form, and the cash advance.

[[Ruth said, ]]@Ruth"Is that online lender licensed in Arizona?"

@Terrence"I don't know."

[[Ruth said, ]]@Ruth"Check before you do anything. Since 2010, a consumer loan that charges more than 36% a year has been against the law in this state. Title loans are the exception. They have their own caps."

@Terrence"So the title shop is legal, and the flyer might not be."

[[Ruth said, ]]@Ruth"Legal isn't the same as cheap. Here's a fourth way. We make small loans to members: $500 at 18% a year, paid back over six months. That comes to $87.76 a month."

Terrence wrote all four in a column down the page. @Terrence"So if I put them all on the same clock, one month each, I can see which one costs the least."

[[Ruth said, ]]@Ruth"That's the job. Do it tonight, and come see me Thursday."

That night, he laid four things on the kitchen table: the orange flyer, the unsigned form, his Visa statement, and his page of notes from the call. Every one of them would get him to $500 by Saturday. The flyer said fourteen days, the form said one month, the Visa said one year, and Ruth had said six months. No two of them told time the same way.

He tapped each one with his pencil. @Terrence"Same $500. So what does each one really cost me?"
