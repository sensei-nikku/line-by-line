## Track 0: Start Here

This is the audio guide for Week 5, Day 1: Reality Check, Collect. Each part of your packet has its own short track. Play the one for the part you're on. You don't have to listen to them all in a row.

Today has one job: collect your numbers. Look it up, write it down, move on.

There are three rules for today.

Rule one: the first good answer is good enough. You're not hunting for the perfect apartment or the perfect car. You're finding one real one.

Rule two: every number you look up gets a source row. Who published it, what page or item, and today's date. That's it.

Rule three: when a card asks a yes-or-no question, answer it. If the answer is no, write zero and skip ahead. Skipping isn't cheating. It's the instructions.

The gray, slanted writing in your packet is Jordan's. Use his row as an example of what goes in each box. Codes in brackets, like MB, are numbers you'll copy into tomorrow's packet.

Your finish line today is four boxes. S, your salary. MB, your monthly take-home. HB, your housing. And TB, getting around.

Stuck for more than a few minutes? First, check the Stuck card nearest you. Still stuck? Use the help booth. Make a tally mark, then ask. Each mark adds $10 a month to your budget, like paying a professional. That's not a punishment. It's how real life works.

Start with the Plan card, then play the Plan track.

## Track 1: The Plan Card

The Plan card is copying, not deciding. Your launch packet already has your decisions. Get it out now.

Copy your city. Then everyone in your household: you, a partner if you have one, your kids, and any adult who lives with you or who you help pay for.

Then your MIT column. That's the column name from the MIT page, like "1 adult, 0 children."

Then check the boxes: adults you support, who works, whether you rent, buy, or own, how many bedrooms, and how you get around.

Here's Jordan's. Phoenix. Just him. 1 adult, 0 children. No one he supports. He works. He rents a one-bedroom. No car. He takes the light rail and the bus.

Changed your mind since the launch packet? That's fine. Write today's decision. Just decide in one minute, not ten.

## Track 2: Part 1, Income

Part 1 is income. You'll fill five cards, 1A through 1E. The goal is one number: MB, your monthly take-home.

Card 1A is your job's pay. Scan the code, or search "CareerOneStop Salary Finder." In the Occupation box, type your job, and pick the closest title from the list. Closest is fine. In the Location box, type your city and state. Click Search.

Find the row for your area and read the Median, yearly. Median means the middle. Half the people in that job make more, and half make less.

Write the number in the box. Then fill in the source row: CareerOneStop, the job title you picked, and today's date.

Jordan's job title is first-line supervisors, transportation and material moving, in Phoenix. The median is $64,990 a year.

If the site says "no data available," or only shows an hourly wage, look at the Stuck card right below.

[PAUSE: Do card 1A.]

Card 1B is your partner's pay. First, the yes-or-no question: does your partner work? No partner, or they don't work? Write $0 and go to 1C.

If they do work, use the same tool with their job. Or use their real pay: what's on one paycheck, times the number of paychecks they get in a year.

[PAUSE: Do card 1B.]

Card 1C is your household salary. Add your pay, your partner's pay, and any side job. For a side job, take the hours a month, times the hourly pay, times 12.

Jordan works 20 hours a month at the diner, for $20 an hour. Times 12, that's $4,800. His household salary, S, is $69,790.

[PAUSE: Do card 1C.]

Card 1D is take-home pay. This is where taxes come out. Scan the code, or search "SmartAsset income tax calculator."

For Household Income, type your S from card 1C. For Location, type your city. For Deduction Type, pick Standard. Any retirement boxes get zero.

Filing status: if you have a partner, pick Married Filing Jointly. If you don't have a partner, but you support a child or another adult, pick Head of Household. Everyone else picks Single. Have kids, or an adult you support? Open Advanced Options and add them as dependents.

Copy the five numbers it gives you: federal tax, FICA tax, state tax, local tax, and take-home pay. You don't need to understand every tax today. Just copy them.

[PAUSE: Do card 1D.]

Card 1E is your monthly take-home. Take your yearly take-home and divide it by 12. Then add any money that comes in without taxes, like money another adult in your home chips in each month. If there's none, add zero.

Jordan's monthly take-home is $4,691. That's MB. You'll use it all week.

[PAUSE: Do card 1E.]

Then play the Part 2 track.

## Track 3: Part 2, Housing

Part 2 is housing. The goal is HB, your housing budget.

First, look at your Plan card. Are you renting, buying, or do you already own? Only do the cards for your choice.

If you're renting, do card 2A-R. Find one place for rent in your city on Zillow, Apartments.com, or Zumper. It needs an address and a posted rent.

For bedrooms, count one for you, or for you and a partner. Add your kids, and any adult you support who lives with you. Then take the first listing that fits. Don't scroll for twenty minutes looking for the perfect one.

Then write any monthly fees on that same listing, like parking or pet rent. If there are none, write zero.

Jordan found a one-bedroom near the light rail for $1,195 a month.

Renters, skip card 2A-B.

If you're buying, or you already own, do card 2A-B. Already own? Copy the total payment from your mortgage statement.

Buying? Find one home for sale in your city on Zillow or Redfin. On the listing, find the monthly payment calculator. Set the down payment to 3.5%. Then copy each line: principal and interest, mortgage insurance, property taxes, home insurance, and HOA fees. Add them up. That's your monthly payment.

Then do the "cash up front" line. Take the price, times 0.065. That's the money you'd need on the day you buy. Don't have it? That's normal. Tomorrow, saving it can become your goal, and you rent until then. If that's you, do card 2A-R too.

[PAUSE: Do card 2A-R or 2A-B.]

Next is the Check card. Would a landlord or a lender approve you?

If you're renting, take your S and divide it by 36. That's about the most rent a landlord will approve.

If you're buying, take your S, divide it by 12, then multiply by 0.31. That's about the biggest house payment a lender will approve.

If your payment is under your limit, check yes. If it's over, go back and pick a cheaper listing. Do it now, not tomorrow.

Jordan can rent up to $1,939. His rent is $1,195, so he's approved. He also priced a house. It came to $2,541 a month, but his lender limit is $1,803. Not approved. That's why he rents.

[PAUSE: Do the Check card.]

Card 2B is insurance and utilities. This one is easy: circle what you pay.

Circle renters insurance if you rent. Circle electric. If you live outside Arizona, scan the EIA code and find your state's row. Circle water and trash, unless your rent includes them. Homeowners always pay water and trash. Circle internet. Circle gas only if your home has gas.

Then add the circled numbers.

[PAUSE: Do card 2B.]

Card 2C is your housing budget. Add your payment, your fees, and your utilities. If you have a roommate who isn't family, subtract their share last.

Jordan's housing budget is $1,445. That's HB.

[PAUSE: Do card 2C.]

Then play the Part 3 track.

## Track 4: Part 3, Getting Around

Part 3 is getting around. The goal is TB, your transportation budget. There are two paths: cars, and bus, rail, or rides. Lots of households use both.

Card 3A is for cars. Yes or no: does your household have a car? If not, write zero in cards 3A through 3D, and skip to 3E.

If you do, find one real listing for each car, on Cars.com, CarMax, or Autotrader. Write the website, the year, make, and model, the price, and the MPG. MPG means miles per gallon.

Then the payment. Take the price, divide by 1,000, then multiply by $23.23. That's a five-year used-car loan.

The model in your packet is a 2019 Honda Civic, for $17,995. The payment comes out to $418 a month.

Car already paid off? The payment is zero. But keep going. You still pay to run it.

[PAUSE: Do card 3A.]

Cards 3B through 3D are the cost of running each car. Use one column per car.

Insurance: in Arizona, use $210 a month.

Miles per month: not sure? Use 1,000.

Gas price: in Arizona, use $4.53 a gallon.

Fuel is your miles, divided by the MPG, times the gas price.

Maintenance and registration is your miles times 11 cents, plus $30.

Outside Arizona? Search "average car insurance" and your state. Then search "AAA gas prices" and your state.

[PAUSE: Do cards 3B to 3D.]

Card 3E is bus, rail, and rides. Yes or no: does anyone in your household ride transit, or take rideshares? If not, write zero and go to 3F.

If they do, take the monthly pass price, times the number of riders. Valley Metro's monthly pass is $64. Then add rideshares: trips a month, times the cost of one trip.

Jordan has one pass. He also takes two rides a month to Mesa, at $28 each. That's $120 total.

[PAUSE: Do card 3E.]

Card 3F is your transportation budget. Copy your car payments, your running costs, and your transit and rides. Then add them.

Jordan's transportation budget is $120. That's TB.

[PAUSE: Do card 3F.]

Then play the last track.

## Track 5: Wrap Up

Last stop: the So Far card. Take your monthly take-home, MB. Subtract your housing, HB. Subtract getting around, TB.

What's left has to pay for food, health, your phone, savings, and fun. That's tomorrow. Jordan has $3,126 left.

Then answer one question: which number surprised you most today? Write one or two sentences. There's no wrong answer.

Last is the Done card. Check off each box you have, with its source. Missing one? That's okay. Write down what's missing, and finish it first thing tomorrow.

You looked it up, you wrote it down, and you moved on. That's the whole job today. Nice work.
