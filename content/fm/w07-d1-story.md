## Track 0: The Minimum

In late August, the school district sent Terrence to rebuild a cooling tower at an elementary school on the far west side of Phoenix. His shift started at five thirty in the morning. The first bus that could get him there left after five, and the trip took an hour and a half with two transfers.

So every morning, he stood on his front step in the dark and called a ride. Each one cost about $26, and each one went on his Copper State Visa. In the afternoons, when the buses were running, he took the long way home.

He still had no car. The disputes he had filed with the three credit bureaus were still open. Ruth Okafor, the loan officer at Valley Credit Union, had told him to wait for the results before he applied for a car loan.

On the twentieth of September, his new statement came in the mail. Terrence cleared the kitchen table, opened his notebook, and went down the statement line by line.

[[The statement said: *Closing date: September 18. New balance: $1,040. Minimum payment due: $35. Payment due date: October 13. Credit limit: $1,200. Purchase APR: 23.99%.*]]{{The closing date was September eighteenth, and the new balance was $1,040. The minimum payment was $35, due by October thirteenth. His credit limit was still $1,200, and the APR was still 23.99%.}}

He counted the rides in the list of charges. There were twenty-five of them, and together they came to $650. The other $390 was groceries and his phone bill, the same things he paid off every month.

Last month, the box at the bottom of the statement had held a warning and nothing else. This month, it held numbers.

[[The box said: *Minimum Payment Warning. If you make no additional charges using this card and each month you pay only the minimum payment, you will pay off the balance shown on this statement in about 4 years, and you will end up paying an estimated total of $1,594. If you pay $41 each month, you will pay off the balance in 3 years and pay an estimated total of $1,476, a savings of $118.*]]{{The box said that if he made no new charges and paid only the minimum, the balance would take about 4 years to pay off. He would pay about $1,594 in all. If he paid $41 a month instead, it would take 3 years and cost about $1,476, a savings of $118.}}

Terrence already had a plan. He had $1,800 in a savings account at Valley, money he had been putting away for a car since spring. A bigger down payment meant a smaller car loan. If he paid only the $35, the car fund stayed whole.

On his lunch break the next day, he called the credit union from the boiler room.

Ruth picked up on the second ring. @Ruth"This is Ruth."

@Terrence"Ruth, it's Terrence Whitfield. I've got a balance on the Visa this month, $1,040, mostly rides to work. I figure I pay the $35 and leave my car money alone."

[[Ruth said, ]]@Ruth"What does the box at the bottom of the statement say?"

@Terrence"About four years at the minimum, and a total of $1,594."

[[Ruth said, ]]@Ruth"Then every $26 ride ends up costing you about $40. That's what the minimum buys you."

Terrence ran his pencil down the page. @Terrence"So if I send $35, how much of it actually comes off what I owe?"

[[Ruth said, ]]@Ruth"Work it out tonight. The interest comes out first. Whatever is left over is what you paid down."

@Terrence"And if I pay more than the minimum?"

[[Ruth said, ]]@Ruth"Then less of every dollar goes to interest, and you finish sooner. That's the math part. There's a credit part too."

She let that sit for a second. @Ruth"Your next closing date is October eighteenth. Whatever you owe that day is what the bureaus see. If it's $1,040 on $1,700 of credit limits, that's 61%, and that's the same month you want to apply for the car."

Terrence shut his notebook. @Terrence"So if I keep the car fund, the card costs me interest, and my score takes a hit right when I apply. And if I pay the card, my down payment shrinks."

[[Ruth said, ]]@Ruth"Same money. Different day. Run the numbers both ways before the thirteenth."

That night, Terrence opened his notebook to a clean page. At the top, he wrote two numbers, $35 and $1,040. Under them, he listed the twenty-five rides, one per line, $26 each.

He looked at the two numbers for a long time. @Terrence"So what does the minimum really cost me, and what does paying more buy?"
