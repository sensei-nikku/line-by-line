## Track 1: Plan It

Start with the Must Have card. It's your poster checklist. Read it now, but don't check anything off yet. You check each item when it's on the poster, not before.

There are three poster rules. Numbers big enough to read from six feet away. Every number has a label. And no number without a dollar sign or a percent sign.

Next is the Labels card. It's wording you can copy straight onto your poster. Fill in the blanks with your own numbers. Jordan's versions are next to each one.

For example: "I'm a warehouse assistant manager in Phoenix, earning $69,790 a year." "Housing takes 31% of my take-home." "My biggest bill: housing, $1,445 a month."

## Track 2: The Bar Chart

Your bar chart is one bar, 20 inches tall. The whole bar is your take-home pay. Each pile gets a piece of it.

Here's the math. Every 1% of your take-home is 0.2 inches. So to find inches, take each pile's percent and divide by 5. Your percents are on your Final Budget Card.

Do the small piles first: Discretionary, then Lifestyle, then Goals, then Security. Add those four together. Essentials gets whatever is left: 20 minus that total. That way, the bar always comes out to exactly 20 inches.

Here's Jordan's. Discretionary is 3%, so 0.6 inches. Lifestyle is 10%, so 2 inches. Goals is 21%, so 4.2 inches. Security is 7%, so 1.4 inches. Together that's 8.2 inches. So Essentials gets 20 minus 8.2, which is 11.8 inches.

[PAUSE: Do the bar math on the Bar card.]

To draw it, use your ruler. Draw the full 20-inch bar in pencil first. Then measure from the top down: Discretionary, Lifestyle, Goals, Security. Essentials is the rest, at the bottom.

Give each pile its own color, and make a key. Next to each section, write its dollars and its percent.

Then play the next track for your reflection.

## Track 3: Your Reflection

The reflection goes at the bottom of your poster. It's 6 to 10 sentences. Draft it in the packet first, then copy it.

Answer three questions, with 2 or 3 sentences each. Each one has a starter if you want it.

Question 1: What surprised you? You can start with, "I was surprised that." Jordan wrote: "A $94 minimum payment felt small. Paying the card off still takes $993 a month for four months."

Question 2: What did you change to balance it, or where did the extra go? You can start with, "To balance my budget, I."

Question 3: What is one real-life decision this changed for you? You can start with, "Because of this, in real life I will."

There's no wrong answer. Honest beats fancy.

[PAUSE: Draft your reflection.]

Then play the last track.

## Track 4: Build and Check

Now build it. Pencil first, in the order of your layout. Title and your name at the top. Then your magic-wand life and your income. Then the bar chart, your balance, and your checks. The reflection goes at the bottom, and the sources box goes in the bottom right corner.

Check each item on the Must Have card as it goes on the poster.

When the pencil looks right, go over it in marker.

[PAUSE: Build your poster.]

Last, the gallery check. Trade posters with a neighbor. They get 10 seconds to find each thing on the 10 Seconds card: the city and the job, the monthly take-home, the biggest pile, the $0 balance, the housing share, and the sources.

Anything they couldn't find in 10 seconds needs to be bigger or clearer. Write down one thing they said to fix, and fix it.

[PAUSE: Do the gallery check and make your fix.]

Before you leave, write your name on the back of the poster, and put all three packets in your folder.
